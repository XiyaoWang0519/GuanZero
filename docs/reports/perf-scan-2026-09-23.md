# Performance scan of training and evaluation, and the first fixes

Status: September 23, 2026. A whole-stack scan (nine subsystems, each finding
checked by an independent skeptic, plus four gap scans) ranked what slows
Stage B/C training and evaluation. The five changes below were then made,
each verified **identical to the previous code on CPU** (choices, draws,
buffers and league state; see the caveat on reference log-probabilities
below). No GPU was
rented, so every pod figure is an estimate from logs and code.

## Where the time went

- **League PPO arms pay a full separate act path per opponent network.**
  Each Stage B opponent spec (snapshots, imports, `frozen:`/`follow:`
  targets) re-uploaded features as float32, re-ran the frozen M1 for pruning,
  used masked phase heads (about 12 device syncs per call) and copied back on
  its own. With the B11 cap of 16 active models that is about 13 calls per
  step; B11 main ran 10.1 s per update (13k decisions/s), 86% of it collect.
- **B11 phase 1 ran 4 engine threads per arm, not 16.** The kit computed
  threads with GNU `nproc` after exporting `OMP_NUM_THREADS=4`, and `nproc`
  honours it. `nproc --all` is no fix either: it ignores cpusets and quotas.
- **The engine generated every decision's moves twice** (`Engine::apply`
  auto-pass, then `pending()`), and `pending()` zero-filled about 30 MB
  serially per step at 2,048 environments although the encoders overwrite
  every element.
- **Evaluation ran the M1 pruning forward on every decision**, although 92%
  of play decisions have at most `top_k` candidates and keep them all.
  Batch-1 forwards are 80% of evaluation time.

Fused reference scores are computed in a larger batch than before. On CPU
that is bitwise equal except when the old batch had a single row: a step with
exactly one learner row then stores `ref_logp` that differs by float noise
(up to 5e-5 at temperature 0.02, found by the review; choices were
unaffected in every run). On CUDA the same holds more broadly, and near-ties
may flip: statistically equivalent, not bitwise. One error path changed in
evaluation: a checkpoint whose frozen reference produces non-finite scores
now raises only on decisions that need pruning, because the others no longer
run the reference.

An independent read-only review (three reviewers: league semantics and the
CUDA path, engine and eval semantics, house rules and tests) re-ran the
equivalence on its own cases (16 league configurations including actor
processes, a pinned follow exploiter, learned tribute and foreign references;
17 VecEnv configurations with explicit deals and forks; about 515k eval
decisions including learned tribute; `crossplay`, `stage_b_baseline`,
`pruning_audit` and the DanLM arena end to end) and found no defect. Its
mutation testing found test gaps, now closed: `StageBPolicy.choose` against
`act`, the sharing gates, and tribute decisions in `PrunedPolicy.select`.

Also corrected: `train/ppo_actors.py` and `stage-b-throughput.md` said there
is no policy lag. That holds for steps collected within an update; with
rounds of about 70 decisions against 64-step rollouts, about half of the
trained samples come from carried-over rounds drawn under the previous
weights, in actor mode and in-process alike.

## Expected effect on the next pod (estimate)

For a B11-like host (three arms on one RTX 4090), the scan projects a
main-like league arm at about 10.1 → 5.4-6.7 s per update and the pod from
about 54k to about 71-87k decisions/s with these changes and correct thread
counts. The per-call cost on the 4090 is dominated by syncs and launches that
the CPU measurement cannot show, so this needs the pod check below. Milestone
evaluation on the Mac falls from about 60-65 to about 28-32 minutes from the
eval change alone.

## Next, in order

1. On the next pod, before the arms start (about 5 minutes): host facts
   (`python -m infra.cpu_budget --facts`), engine thread scaling on a frozen
   arm, and the per-spec league cost against a steady cap-16 pool, alone and
   with a co-running arm. This confirms the GPU-side estimates.
2. Batched duplicate evaluator over `VecEnv` (a prototype measured 13-19x per
   process with identical per-deal results for deterministic policies): the
   milestone evaluation to about 8-12 minutes.
3. Learner: gather minibatches from the uint8 staging copy and keep rollout
   data on the device (about 0.1-0.5 s per update).
4. The remaining engine work (movegen feasibility pre-checks, encoder bit
   operations; about 2.5-3x engine speed, bitwise) before C3 search.
5. DanLM arena: per-seat KV cache and memos (73.5 → about 45 s per 1,000
   deals together with the eval change).
6. `pytest -n 4` (check.sh 90 → about 30 s).

Deferred: asynchronous actors (changes results, small gain while arms share
a GPU), CUDA graphs and `torch.compile` (1-3% after the above), `-march=native`
(changes the styled bot's float results).
