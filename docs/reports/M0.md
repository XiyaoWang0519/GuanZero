Candidates per decision, which task 5 asks to record:

| Mode | Mean | p50 | p90 | p99 | Max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Canonical | 9.9 | 3 | 18 | 106 | 1,096 |
| Full | 23.7 | 4 | 26 | 426 | 4,380 |

Two consequences for DESIGN.md section 8.6, whose load estimate this replaces.
The mean candidate count is 9.9, not the 30 assumed there, because most
decisions are follows with very few options; the network's per-candidate cost
is therefore about a third of the estimate. And the engine is comfortably not
the bottleneck: one core sustains 190,000 decisions per second, so a 16-vCPU
node feeds a GPU learner with room to spare.

## 3. Corrections to the specification

**`RULES.md` 11.2 and 14.5, the canonical-mode guarantee.** The document
required that every full-mode action survive as the same abstract action or as
a stronger reading of the same cards. That cannot hold alongside the three
reductions the same section defines. With a wild card in hand, every card
multiset that reads as a low full house also reads as a higher one, so
`prune_dominated_readings` removes the low key outright rather than leaving it
on some other multiset. The guarantee is now stated as what the engine
actually provides and the tests enforce: no type is ever lost, and for every
type the best reading in canonical mode is the best reading in full mode. What
can disappear is a deliberately weak declaration of a type the player can also
play strongly.

**One engine bug worth recording.** Straight flushes need their own
suit-restricted generation pass. Picking natural cards suit-blind let the
minimal-wild rule take a card of the wrong suit, so a hand that could complete
a flush with a wild card lost the straight flush entirely in canonical mode —
a whole bomb-class play, silently absent. The canonical-mode invariant test is
what caught it.

**AddressSanitizer does not run on this host.** It deadlocks before `main`,
inside its own runtime initialisation: `AsanInitInternal` calls
`InitializeShadowMemory`, dyld shared-cache iteration allocates through the
intercepted malloc, re-enters `AsanInitFromRtl` and spins on a static spin
mutex. A sample of the hung process shows no frames from this project at all.
The same host also ships a CommandLineTools SDK whose `.tbd` stubs the linker
cannot parse, so the prerelease toolchain is the common cause.

UBSan runs normally and is clean: 100,002 rounds and 7,354,157 decisions in
canonical mode, 30,000 rounds and 2,256,606 decisions in full mode, plus the
unit tests, all with `halt_on_error=1` and no report. `GD_SANITIZE` now takes
an explicit sanitizer list so that `undefined` can be selected alone, and the
CI job keeps running address and undefined together on Linux, which is where
ASan coverage comes from.

## 4. OpenGuanDan parity

The simulator is reachable and its `guandan-java/` bridge exposes the Java move
generator to Python directly, which is far cheaper than the WebSocket route.
1,000 matches of random play were logged and replayed through our engine under
the `ogd` rule profile.

Result: 1,783,202 decisions and 13,341 round ends compared. Every finishing
order, every level change, every tribute movement and every action the
simulator's players chose matched. One class of difference remains, on 3,784
decisions, or 0.21%, and in every case our legal set is a strict superset.

The cause is isolated in `RULES.md` 13.2 and reproduced by probing the move
generator directly: the simulator does not enumerate a wild card standing for
something weaker than what the card already is. It is documented rather than
emulated, because our set never misses a play and nothing depends on
byte-exact action lists.

Parity checks closed: O1, O2, O3, O4, O5, O6, O8, O9, and a new O11 for the
wild-card behaviour above. The house and `ogd` profiles now differ in six
named config fields, all in `cpp/include/gd/config.h`. The notable ones: the
simulator routes a double tribute by seat geometry rather than by card power,
it does not count a lost level-A round as a failed attempt, and its reset lands
on the fourth failure rather than the third.

## 5. Finding that affects M2 and M3

**The OpenGuanDan repository ships no agents and no weights.** DESIGN.md
section 2 and 9.2 assume seven built-in agents usable as an external ladder.
The repository contains a rules server, an Electron client and the move
generator jar; a search for weight files finds nothing, and the strings in the
native binaries that look like agent names are V8 and Electron symbols. There
is also no LICENSE file anywhere in it, so nothing from it is vendored here and
the adapter locates a local clone through `OGD_ROOT`.

The M2 and M3 gates are stated as win rates against DanZero, SDMC and GS2. As
things stand those opponents are not available to play against. The fallback in
DESIGN.md's risk table now becomes the plan: train a DanZero-style baseline
with our own pipeline and use it as the reference point, while the published
win rates stay as calibration only. This should be settled before M2 starts,
since it changes what the gates can mean.

## 6. Repository state

New since the scaffold: `gd_core` (cards, readings, move generation, the state
machine, the feature encoder, heuristic bots, the vectorized environment), the
pybind11 module, the property fuzzer, the throughput benchmark, the
OpenGuanDan adapter with its trace logger, probes and replay diff, and the
test suites. 129 Python tests, 52 C++ cases and 14 oracle groups.

Two practical notes for whoever runs this next. The build asks `xcrun` for the
macOS SDK, because the CommandLineTools SDK on this host ships `.tbd` stubs
with an `arm64e.x1` slice the linker cannot parse, which breaks CMake's own
compiler check. And the traces are large, about 1.2 GB for 1,000 matches,
because the simulator's full-mode action lists are verbose; they live outside
the repository and `trace_logger.py` regenerates them in about two minutes.

## 7. What M1 starts from

The interfaces M1 needs are in place: `VecEnv.pending()` and `step()` with
zero-copy observation and candidate buffers, `DealSpec` for duplicate deals,
`fork` for the counterfactual tribute branches of DESIGN.md 8.3, and
`RoundResult.seat_return` carrying the DMC target. The encoder's block layout
is pinned by golden tests, so a feature change that shifts a block fails
loudly instead of quietly retraining on shifted inputs.
