# RTX 4090 training and evaluation performance validation

Two supervised Runpod RTX 4090 sessions on 2026-09-24 tested the September 24
local performance pass on CUDA, tuned a B9 training recipe for the first host,
and measured two additional inference/evaluation changes. The two pods were
deleted after their results were copied and hash-verified. Runpod reported zero
pods and `$0/hour` current spend at the final check.

## What changed

- `train/model.py` factors the first static phase-head fusion projection into a
  state part computed once per row and an action part computed per candidate.
  It uses this path for at least 64 candidates and at least 12 candidates per
  row; sparse PPO learner rows, tiny batches, dynamic heads, and autocast retain
  the original calculation. Model weights and checkpoint keys are unchanged.
- `eval/batched.py` asks `gd.VecEnv` to omit observation/action encoding when
  every policy is scripted. Model policies still receive encoded features.
- `train/configs/b9-exploit-m1-rtx4090.json` is an optional frozen-arm preset
  with two Torch threads and 16 engine threads. On the first pod's 17.85-CPU
  quota, two had higher median throughput than four. The paired B9 frozen and
  league experiment configs remain unchanged and differ only in opponent;
  eight Torch threads were not directly measured, and concurrent arms need
  their own CPU budget.
- `infra/runpod.py` includes the HTTP status, when available, in an ambiguous
  creation error. Ambiguous POSTs were reconciled against the provider's pod
  list before another create attempt; the CLI never blindly retried one.

## Training measurements

The complete Stage B PPO benchmark used the pretrained M1 policy and B2
critic, 2,048 environments, 64 rollout steps, four epochs and 8,192-row
minibatches. It performed real CUDA rollout and optimizer updates. On the first
pod, with eight engine threads and two Torch threads, two eight-update runs per
variant gave these medians:

| Source | Decisions/s median | Collection per eight updates | Learning per eight updates |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Committed source | 34,349 | 13.23 s | 18.41 s |
| September 24 local pass | 46,781 | 9.42 s | 13.90 s |
| Local pass plus initial fusion split | 42,011 | 9.74 s | 15.63 s |

The local pass was about 1.35–1.37× faster than committed source in the adjacent
comparison, but these short runs changed trajectories and sometimes performed
252 rather than 256 optimizer minibatches. Host speed also varied markedly:
the unchanged committed source ranged from 27,894 to 40,803 decisions/s. The
initial fusion split's end-to-end effect cannot be inferred from this table.

Thread scaling on the initial split at two Torch threads, with two six-update
runs per setting, gave median 49,443 / 56,320 / 59,954 decisions/s for 4 / 8 /
16 engine threads. At 16 engine threads and a 32,768 candidate chunk, two Torch
threads gave 60,708 decisions/s versus 54,783 with four (two runs each).
Increasing the candidate chunk to 262,144 changed the two-thread median to
61,139 decisions/s, only a 0.7% difference, so the default chunk remains
32,768. These settings should be remeasured on hosts with other CPU quotas or
with concurrent training arms.

On the second pod, eight complete PPO trials alternated old and split scoring
as local/split/split/local twice, with 16 engine threads and two Torch threads.
The range was 31,103–57,328 decisions/s for the old model and 38,819–54,429
for split. It swung even within one ABBA cycle. The split's full-training
speedup remains **unproven**; the table's apparent medians would attribute host
drift to code. We used a same-process, fixed real minibatch to resolve the
learner path instead.

The fixed PPO minibatch contained 8,192 rows and 64,124 pruned candidates
(7.83/row). Over 40 synchronized ABBA cycles of full minibatch loss, backward
and gradient clipping, the old path took 15.754 ms median versus 15.904 ms for
the split, with split faster in only 6 of 40 paired cycles. A separate sweep
selected complete real PPO candidate rows at four densities and held the row
count at 8,192:

| Actual candidates/row | Median paired old/split time | Split faster cycles |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 7.47 | 0.989× | 4/10 |
| 11.66 | 1.001× | 5/10 |
| 15.74 | 1.042× | 7/10 |
| 23.99 | 1.065× | 8/10 |

Rows above eight candidates were sampled with replacement when fewer than
8,192 distinct real rows qualified; the raw JSON records eligible and unique
row counts. The 12-candidate dispatch boundary is a conservative crossover,
not a claim of a globally optimal threshold. With the final gate, the actual
7.77-candidate PPO minibatch had exactly equal old/new scores and choices; 20
paired cycles gave old/gated median time ratio 1.0008. This confirms the sparse
learner keeps its original calculation. The isolated unpruned GPU scorer at
18,770 candidates over 1,024 play rows took 1.828 ms old versus 1.054 ms split
(1.73×), with maximum FP32 score difference `5.96e-7` and zero changed choices.
The forward/backward microbenchmark took 6.429 versus 3.904 ms (1.65×); these
component timings are not whole PPO training throughput.

## Evaluation measurements

All comparisons used the same seeded deals and compared the complete score
digest, not only summary metrics.

| Evaluation on first pod | Baseline median | Optimized median | Throughput gain | Outcome |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| M1 model vs greedy, 64 duplicate deals, one engine thread | 1.326 s | 1.108 s | 1.20× | Identical score digest |
| M1 model vs greedy, 64 duplicate deals, 16 threads | 1.319 s | 1.123 s | 1.18× | Identical score digest |
| Styled vs greedy, 5,000 duplicate deals, one thread | 7.510 s | 4.998 s | 1.50× | Identical score digest |
| Styled vs greedy, 5,000 duplicate deals, 16 threads | 4.666 s | 2.719 s | 1.72× | Identical score digest |

The model score digest was
`8d5999d50b970681121bb065f28852ff6287c874a98d98d7a58704b16866bb67`
in all six initial runs and in the final gated-code CUDA run. The scripted
digest was `5573eb65180d4e03fde56ca145185f500aa50537b7e081dc6d86b368304b16aa`
in all eight runs. The 16-thread model comparison had only one run per
variant. These are fixed-workload speed/equivalence results, not playing-
strength evidence.
